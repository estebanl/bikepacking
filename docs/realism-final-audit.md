# Preliminary rendered realism audit — 2026-10-03

Independent browser inspection of the existing static production export at `http://127.0.0.1:3011`. This is a visual audit, not a physical-fit certification or a claim of exact manufacturer CAD. No rendering code was edited during this audit. Final independent review remains required after improvements.

## Baseline inspected

Chromium with software WebGL, desktop 1440×1000 and mobile 390×1000. For both Stigmata M and Blur M: Half Frame Bag 2.3L, front Top Tube Bag 0.8L, bundled medium Bar Cage bag, axle/UDH hardware. Stigmata adds Carbon Rack and 5L/10L Mini Panniers; Blur adds alloy CargoPack with pannier mounts. These are complete products, without a display full of selected replacement parts. Stigmata capacity 29.1L; Blur 32.1L. The Blur frame-bag combination remains unverified around its suspension.

Screenshots are executor-local in `/workspace/bikepacking-evidence/realism-final-before/`: each model has `desktop`, `mobile`, `side`, `cockpit`, `rear`, and `detail` PNGs. Camera presets and zoom were exercised. The detail captures are modest zoom views; they should not be described as macro images.

## Highest-impact improvements

1. **Give soft luggage a soft silhouette and actual closure shape.** In both cockpit screenshots the medium Bar Cage bag is a long, bevelled rectangular brick with flat rectangular end plates. The rear CargoPack similarly resembles a hard storage case; panniers have straight, rigid slab faces. Small stitched lines cannot overcome those silhouettes. Replace the generic extruded prism for bar rolls with an elliptical/rounded cross-section, compressed roll ends and webbing following that section. Give CargoPack/pannier side panels modest fullness, curved corners and a visibly folded roll closure. Preserve sourced/estimated envelope bounds, existing support contact, socket poses and single-count assembly mass. Starting points: `src/components/canvas/equipment/EquipmentModel.tsx:138` (`PanelShell`), `:457` (shared shell), `:514` (bar roll details), `:549` (roll closures). A separate envelope-aware shell is preferable to arbitrarily scaling whole assemblies.

2. **Replace tire “hairs” with tread that reads as rubber.** The Blur cockpit and desktop silhouettes have short thin lines protruding around a largely smooth torus. Stigmata has the same visual treatment despite its different use. Use inexpensive instanced, low-profile solid tread blocks: broader spaced shoulder/centre blocks on the MTB, much smaller closely spaced features on gravel. Keep the measured outer radius and tire width stable. Blend the tan sidewall into the carcass rather than presenting it as an independent round ring. Starting point: `src/components/canvas/WheelMesh.tsx:37`–`:74`. This is visible even at the normal desktop camera, so it ranks above adding tiny lettering.

3. **Make cockpit controls recognizable instead of exposed rods.** Blur close views show a swept bar with grips and thin straight brake-lever rods, but very little lever housing/clamp definition. The Stigmata hood/drop transition reads as thick black tubes rather than a hood body, separate brake blade and wrapped bar. Add simple housings, believable tapered blades and restrained grip/tape texture; retain the established bar width and stem coordinates. Avoid invented branded component detail. Starting point: `src/components/canvas/CockpitMesh.tsx:25` onward. Current bag/cradle supports should remain connected when the cockpit changes.

4. **Separate fabric, rubber, painted frame and hardware through light response.** Black bag panels remain uniformly dark and nearly flat even in close views; their fabric texture/seams do not read at the normal camera. Hard edges and similarly dark buckles reinforce the hard-case impression. Use subtle normal/roughness variation at an appropriate world scale, softer panel shading, slightly differentiated webbing and controlled metal highlights. Reduce reliance on strong ambient fill and the blue rim light; use a broad neutral key and restrained fill so folded fabric is visible without turning black bags grey. Starting points: `EquipmentModel.tsx:20`–`:59`, `:206`–`:220`; `src/components/canvas/ConfiguratorCanvas.tsx:43`–`:72`. Judge at both normal and close views; avoid exaggerated weave or glossy waterproof fabric.

5. **Improve the studio framing and ground treatment.** On the phone the complete bike occupies roughly the middle half of the preview width while a large pale area and diagonal grid remain. Desktop has a substantial empty upper field; cockpit views crop the front tire and lower bike without clearly framing only the intended component. The grid and sharply projected bag shadows compete with the product silhouette. Fit default framing to the bike-plus-luggage bounds with modest margins, keep close-up presets deliberately targeted, and make the floor grid much quieter or optional. Use a soft contact shadow to retain grounding. Starting points: `src/components/canvas/CameraController.tsx:18`–`:30`, `GroundPlane.tsx:6`–`:10`, `ConfiguratorCanvas.tsx:43`–`:72`. Preserve manual orbit/zoom ownership and mobile controls; test the largest rear/front load combinations for clipping.

6. **Do not let a frame bag conceal the defining suspension geometry in the Blur review setup.** The complete-rig Blur screenshots visually bury the shock area behind the generic half-frame shell. That makes the full-suspension bike read more like a hardtail and masks a potentially important unverified fit. For final hero/review evidence choose a compatible illustrative open setup or show both loaded and unloaded suspension detail. If improving the bag shape, account for the shock envelope explicitly rather than hiding it behind an opaque generic slab; do not imply fit approval. Relevant ownership: `src/components/canvas/BagMesh.tsx`, `src/lib/equipmentGeometry.ts`, `src/data/santaCruz.ts` frame-triangle restriction. The current source note already warns about suspension clearance; retain that uncertainty.

## What already reads coherently

Overall wheelbase/frame/wheel proportions distinguish the two bikes reasonably at normal distance. The rigid Stigmata versus suspended Blur fork, tan versus dark tire treatment, drop versus flat bar, and rear attachment systems are recognizable. Recent rack/pannier hook alignment and tube-following straps are materially better than the older captures. Do not replace verified dimensions merely to achieve a more dramatic image.

The drivetrain is present with chainring, cassette and derailleur detail. Close inspection still reads the chain as a smooth silver cable (`DrivetrainMesh.tsx:22`), and a future low-cost instanced-link treatment would help. It is lower priority than the large bag silhouettes, tire treatment and cockpit above.

## Independent after-review — settled runtime, 2026-10-03

Reviewed the frozen changes on fresh runtime port 3018 after the CargoPack floor/deck adjustment. Used the same complete M-size setups above, desktop 1440×1000 and mobile 390×1000, with at least 4.2 seconds settling after every preset/viewport change. Personally inspected the desktop, cockpit, mobile, side and rear evidence, plus an uncovered Blur suspension view. Final evidence is in `/workspace/bikepacking-evidence/realism-final-after/`.

The visual improvement is clear: the Bar Cage bag reads as a rounded roll rather than a rectangular crate; the CargoPack has fuller curved sides and sits on the deck in the side view; solid MTB blocks and finer gravel tread replace the hair-like outlines; hoods, lever bodies and bar wrap are recognizable; the default phone view uses more of the available preview; removing the grid simplifies the silhouette. The same bike/bag configurations retained their displayed mass and capacity. I found no new duplicated bag shells or disconnected cradles in these views. The paired panniers remain outside the rear tire. The uncovered Blur image exposes the shock area that the loaded frame bag obscures.

Remaining limitations are visible and should be stated plainly: panniers, frame bags and top-tube bags still have comparatively rigid, angular shells; the new rolls are smoother and more uniform than loaded fabric; ground shadows remain fairly sharp and graphic; cockpit component shapes are approximate, and the chain still reads as a smooth cable at close range. Cockpit/rear presets deliberately crop the whole bicycle, unlike the default full-rig framing. The loaded Blur frame bag still hides much of the suspension, so the separate uncovered view is useful evidence, not proof that the loaded combination physically fits. These are illustrative-product limitations rather than newly observed blocking attachment regressions. This is a cleaner, more believable configurator, not photorealism or exact manufacturer CAD.
